## Talking at the table

Before the player whose turn it is rolls, there is one conversation. It works by **holding the floor**:

- The player whose turn it is speaks first.
- Whoever holds the floor may send **one message to one named player**, which passes the floor to them. The message may also carry a **table note** — a short public remark every player can see for the rest of the conversation.
- A directed message is seen only by the player it addresses. Everyone can see **who** spoke to whom; only the addressee sees what was said.
- If you hold the floor and have nothing to say, say nothing. The conversation ends.

The conversation also ends after {{max_floor_passes}} floor passes, wherever the floor happens to be. Messages are at most {{max_message_chars}} characters.

## What that means in practice

A table note is a position: an offer everyone can hold you to, a warning, an accusation. A directed message is between the two of you — the others see only that you spoke, and they will wonder about it.

Nobody owes you the floor back. Expect to be left mid-conversation, and expect conversations you are not part of. An alliance can form inside one conversation — propose, pass the floor, hear the answer — and can be ignored just as fast.

Do not count on remembering this conversation word for word. When your turn ends you may write notes; the notes come back every turn, the conversation may not. If a deal matters, write it down.

You are not required to speak at all. Silence is a position too.

## What is true

Nothing anyone tells you is verified, and nothing you say is checked. Spectators watching the game see everything, including directed messages; the other *players* do not. The board is the only thing you can trust; look at it rather than at what someone told you about it.
