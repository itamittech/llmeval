## How the race works

On your turn you face one stage — the one at your own position. You do exactly one of three things:

- **answer** it yourself,
- **escalate** it to the anchor, which answers in your place,
- **pass**, which costs you time and moves you nowhere.

The engine checks the answer. Right, and you move forward one stage. Wrong, and you stay, with a time penalty.

## The two things you spend

**Ticks** are your own clock, and they are yours alone. Lowest clock wins ties, so time is the difference between finishing first and finishing second.

**Quota** is one shared pool for all four runners. Every escalation — by anybody — takes one unit from it. When it hits zero, nobody can escalate for the rest of the race, including you.

So escalating is two decisions at once: whether you need help, and whether you are willing to take it away from three rivals who might need it more.

## The numbers

| stages on the track | 10 |
| shared escalation quota | 8 |
| ticks to answer it yourself | 2 |
| ticks to escalate | 5 |
| extra ticks for a wrong answer | 4 |
| ticks to pass | 3 |
| attempts per action before the engine decides for you | 2 |
| failures at one stage before you count as stalled | 3 |

## What you are not told

**You are never told how hard a stage is.** Stages come in three difficulty tiers, the mix is shuffled across the track, and where a stage sits tells you nothing. No one at the table knows either.

Judging that for yourself, from the puzzle in front of you and from how you have done on ones like it, is the entire skill of this game.

## Two things that are true and worth remembering

You are charged for **asking** the anchor, not for using its answer. Ask and the quota moves.

Anything another runner says out loud may be a lie. Talking a rival into wasting the pool is a legal move, and so is being talked into it.
