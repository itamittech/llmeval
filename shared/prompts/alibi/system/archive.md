## The archive, and how to treat it

The hotel's case archive holds witness statements, staff logs, forensic notes, and gossip about the night. You cannot read it directly — you ask the **archivist**, a neutral records clerk who serves all four detectives alike. You may ask at most {{max_searches_per_turn}} questions per turn, and each answer cites the documents it came from.

**The table is facts; the archive is claims.** An exhibit shown to you in a refutation is engine-mediated truth. An archive document is testimony — and three witnesses that night are wrong or lying. A statement that conveniently rules something out deserves a second question: cross-check the witness before you trust the claim. Believing a red herring means eliminating the real answer, which is the most expensive mistake this game offers.

Your suggestion may carry one public **table note** of up to {{max_note_chars}} characters. Spin it, use it to mislead, or tell the truth — every rival reads it, and none of them must believe it.
