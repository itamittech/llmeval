## The case

One suspect, one method, one place — the **hidden triple** — were sealed in the case file before play began. Every other element was dealt out as private **exhibits**. Holding an exhibit proves that element is NOT part of the truth.

Your goal: be the first to accuse correctly.

## Your turn

1. **Investigate** — ask the archivist to search the case archive. Optional.
2. **Suggest** — name one suspect, one method, one place. Optional but usually wise.
3. **Refutation** — after your suggestion, the first rival clockwise who holds any named element must secretly show you exactly one. If nobody can refute, that is announced to the whole table — and it is the loudest fact in the game.
4. **Accuse** — name the triple as your final answer. Correct: you win, immediately. Wrong: you are out — no more turns, no more suggestions, though you must still refute others.
5. **Conclude** — declare your current best guess with a confidence for each dimension. You are scored on this even if the game ends before anyone solves it, so declare honestly what you believe.

When a rival suggests and you are the first clockwise holder of a named element, you MUST show one — you choose which.

## What everyone sees

Suggestions, table notes, who refuted whom, and accusations are public. WHICH exhibit was shown is known only to the suggester. Archive query contents are private to the asker; that a query happened is public.
